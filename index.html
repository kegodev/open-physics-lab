Foundation

Photoelectric-effect theory

When electromagnetic radiation strikes a suitable metal surface, electrons may be emitted. Each photon carries energy E = hf = hc/λ. An electron can escape only when the photon energy exceeds the material work function, φ.

Photon energyE = hf = hc/λ
Maximum kinetic energyKmax = hf − φ
Stopping potentialeVs = hf − φ
Linear formVs = (h/e)f − φ/e

Predictions to test

  • Below the threshold frequency, no photoelectrons are emitted, regardless of intensity.
  • At fixed frequency, increasing intensity increases photocurrent and saturation current.
  • At fixed frequency, intensity should not materially change the stopping potential.
  • Increasing frequency increases maximum kinetic energy and stopping potential.
  • A graph of Vs against frequency should be linear, with slope h/e.

Instrument realism in this simulator

The virtual meter includes dark current, a session-specific zero offset, random noise, finite resolution and overload. The source includes warm-up drift, aperture scaling, inverse-square distance scaling and wavelength-dependent response. These effects are deliberately modest so that the correct physics remains recoverable through good technique.

Quick reference

How to use the simulator

  1. Select a mode. Learn reveals theoretical values; Laboratory hides direct answers; Assessment randomises hidden apparatus parameters.
  2. Switch on the lamp. Wait for the accelerated warm-up to complete.
  3. Calibrate the current meter. Keep the shutter closed, enable Calibration mode, choose a sensitive range and press Zero current meter.
  4. Choose the optics. Select wavelength, intensity, aperture and source distance.
  5. Open the shutter. Verify that photon energy exceeds the cathode work function.
  6. Measure an I-V curve. Apply negative retarding bias through zero into positive accelerating bias. Record multiple readings.
  7. Estimate stopping potential. Use the zero-current criterion and the recorded sweep, then repeat at several wavelengths.
  8. Analyse. Open Notebook & graphs, calculate the regression and determine Planck's constant and work function.
  9. Document. Write observations and uncertainty sources, then generate and save the practical report.
Important: This is a virtual experiment. Values include simulated noise and offsets. Do not treat a single reading as exact.
